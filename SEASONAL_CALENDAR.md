# Seasonal Business Calendar — Rest Assured Moving LLC

A month-by-month guide to marketing intensity, demand patterns, operational
priorities, and what to focus on each season. Moving labor is seasonal — this
calendar helps you work with the cycle instead of against it.

---

## Annual Demand Overview

```
Jan  Feb  Mar  Apr  May  Jun  Jul  Aug  Sep  Oct  Nov  Dec
 ▁▁   ▁▁   ▂▂   ▃▃   ▅▅   ▇▇   ██   ██   ▅▅   ▃▃   ▁▁   ▁▁
LOW       BUILDING      PEAK SEASON         TAPERING   LOW
```

**Peak season:** May through August (especially July–August)
**Shoulder season:** March–April and September–October
**Slow season:** November through February

---

## January

**Demand level:** Low
**Primary focus:** Setup, systems, and positioning for the year

### Marketing
- Run a quiet "New Year, New Place" campaign — furniture assembly push
  (people buy new furniture post-holidays)
- Lower ad spend; this is not the time to pay peak CPL
- Focus on GBP: add photos from last season's jobs, update any changed hours
- Social: 2 posts/week, content themes — fresh starts, organization, assembly

### Operations
- Review last year's job log: which jobs were most profitable? Which
  customers came from which channels?
- Update rates if warranted (see PRICING_GUIDE.md)
- Equipment audit: replace worn blankets, check dollies and tools
- Re-read BOOKING_WORKFLOW.md and tighten any process gaps from last year
- Update all 2024 and stat placeholders on the website with current figures

### Admin
- File business taxes or prepare records for accountant
- Review and update business insurance — confirm coverage is current
- Confirm business license is active for current year

### KPI to check
- Total jobs completed in prior year
- Average job value
- Review count and average star rating
- Best and worst performing marketing channels

---

## February

**Demand level:** Low
**Primary focus:** Content, SEO, and early-bird positioning

### Marketing
- Publish a new blog post (moving tips, packing guide, assembly guide)
- Begin "Book early for spring" messaging — light, not urgent
- Continue 2 posts/week on social; educational content performs well in slow season
- Research competitors: what are they ranking for, what reviews do they have?

### Operations
- Begin reaching out to referral partners (see REFERRAL_PARTNER_TOOLKIT.md)
  — truck rental dealers, real estate agents, apartment managers
- Practice the quote call process; identify any friction points
- Set availability calendar for spring bookings

### Hiring (if needed)
- If you expect to expand crew size for peak season, now is the time to
  recruit — hire and train before March, not in May when you need them immediately

---

## March

**Demand level:** Building
**Primary focus:** Activate spring campaigns, start converting leads

### Marketing
- Launch spring campaign: "Get ahead of moving season"
- Increase social posting to 3x/week
- Begin boosting top-performing Facebook posts with paid budget ($50–100/month)
- GBP: post twice per week (use GBP post templates from GBP_CONTENT.md)
- Confirm Google Local Services Ads are active and verified (if set up)

### Operations
- Respond to all quote requests within 2 hours during business hours
- Calendar starting to fill for April–May — confirm crew availability
- Equipment fully ready for the season

### Referral partners
- Follow up on February outreach; schedule face-to-face meetings with
  2–3 local real estate agents or truck rental managers

---

## April

**Demand level:** Building strongly
**Primary focus:** Convert quote requests; lock in May–June calendar

### Marketing
- "April movers book May dates" urgency messaging
- Increase social to 4x/week
- Begin Facebook advertising if not already running ($150–300/month)
- Instagram: aim for at least 1 Reel per week

### Operations
- Calendar should be filling for May; confirm crew for known bookings
- Review quote conversion rate — if below 35%, revisit quote response template
  and follow-up timing
- Collect 2–3 Google reviews from April jobs — important to build momentum
  before peak season when review volume rises

### Pricing
- If demand justifies it, raise rates now before peak season
  (easier to raise before season starts than mid-season)

---

## May

**Demand level:** High — peak season begins
**Primary focus:** Maximize bookings; maintain crew quality; collect reviews

### Marketing
- Urgency messaging: "Summer dates filling fast"
- Maximum social frequency (4–5x/week across platforms)
- Facebook and Instagram ads at full budget
- Push hard on review collection — every completed job, every time
- Consider a first-time customer offer if calendar has gaps

### Operations
- All crew confirmed and briefed
- Job site checklist (JOB_SITE_CHECKLIST.md) reviewed with crew
- Booking workflow (BOOKING_WORKFLOW.md) followed exactly — no shortcuts
- Respond to all inquiries within 1 hour during business hours

### Financial
- Peak season cash flow should be positive — track weekly revenue vs. expenses
- If consistently turning away bookings, evaluate adding crew or raising rates

---

## June

**Demand level:** Peak
**Primary focus:** Execute flawlessly; every job is a review and a referral

### Marketing
- Keep ads running; cost-per-lead is higher in summer but lead quality is good
- Post to social but don't spend hours on content — jobs are the priority
- Actively collect reviews: target adding at least 6–8 reviews this month

### Operations
- Pre-job briefings: never skip the walkthrough
- Double-check crew is not overworked — exhausted crew makes mistakes
- Confirm bookings 48 hours out (customers who booked in March sometimes forget)
- Track actual hours vs. estimated hours per job type — data for rate-setting

### Customer experience
- June movers tell their friends about their moves — this is peak referral season
- Leave a referral card with every customer (referral-card-front/back.svg from marketing-collateral)

---

## July

**Demand level:** Peak (busiest month of the year)
**Primary focus:** Operational excellence; manage demand carefully

### Marketing
- Reduce new marketing spend if calendar is full — leads you can't fulfill
  are waste
- Continue collecting reviews actively
- Save content/post ideas for September rather than trying to create during
  the busiest operational period

### Operations
- Some Saturdays this month will be back-to-back jobs — plan logistics carefully
- Crew communication is critical: job times, addresses, any changes
- Consider a part-time helper or additional crew for July weekends if demand warrants
- Any damage incidents: document and address immediately per REVIEW_MANAGEMENT_SYSTEM.md

### Financial
- This month should be the highest revenue month of the year
- Set aside a reserve from July earnings for January–February slow season

---

## August

**Demand level:** Peak, then tapering late in the month
**Primary focus:** Close out peak season strong; begin positioning for fall

### Marketing
- Continue peak messaging through August 15
- After August 15: shift to "September availability opening up" messaging
- Back-to-school content: apartment turnover, studio and 1BR jobs
- Begin targeting apartment complex residents and students

### Operations
- September bookings starting to come in — secure them now
- Review what went well and what needs improvement during peak season
  while it's fresh

---

## September

**Demand level:** Shoulder — still strong, especially early
**Primary focus:** Ride out the shoulder season; set up for a strong close

### Marketing
- "Fall move? Still a great time to book" messaging
- Reduce paid ad spend back to shoulder-season budget
- 3x/week social posting
- Instagram Reels: "assembly tips for fall movers"

### Operations
- Begin equipment maintenance post-peak (blankets, dollies, tools)
- Review hires: if you added crew for summer, determine who continues
  into the slower months
- Update the quote page and website testimonials with reviews collected
  during peak season

---

## October

**Demand level:** Tapering to low
**Primary focus:** Complete the season cleanly; plan for slow months

### Marketing
- Last push for fall moves: "October is a great time to move — no summer rush"
- Begin light "beat the holiday chaos" messaging for early November
- Referral partner check-in: visit truck rental contacts and real estate agents
  to stay top of mind before the holidays

### Operations
- Finalize full-year numbers: total jobs, revenue, review count, channel performance
- Any outstanding invoices should be followed up now
- Equipment winterized/cleaned if needed for storage

---

## November

**Demand level:** Low
**Primary focus:** Slow season planning and preparation

### Marketing
- Minimal paid ad spend
- 2x/week social posting; lower-stakes content (tips, behind-the-scenes)
- Run a quiet "furniture assembly for holiday prep" push
  (people rearranging furniture, assembling new items before family arrives)
- Set up or review the email newsletter template for a January campaign

### Operations
- Deep clean and inventory of all equipment
- Update any outdated content on the website (hours, rates, service area)
- Set targets for next year based on this year's data

---

## December

**Demand level:** Low
**Primary focus:** Rest, admin, and planning

### Marketing
- Minimal or no active advertising
- One social post per week — gratitude/community content performs better
  than sales content in December
- Send a brief year-end email to past customers (use newsletter template):
  thank them, mention you're available in January for furniture assembly

### Operations
- Year-end bookkeeping
- Business license renewal if on calendar year
- Equipment review for new-year purchases
- Set rate structure for next year (see PRICING_GUIDE.md)
- Draft next year's social media content calendar

### Personal
- Take at least one week off. Peak season is demanding; slow season exists
  for a reason.
