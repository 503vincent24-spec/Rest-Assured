# Crew Orientation Guide — Rest Assured Moving LLC

Welcome to the team. This guide covers everything you need to know before
your first job: what we do, how we do it, how to treat customers, and what
makes us different from the average moving crew.

Read it once before your first job. Keep it accessible as a reference.

---

## 1. What We Do (and What We Don't)

Rest Assured Moving LLC is a **moving labor company**. We load, unload,
pack, unpack, and assemble furniture. We do not own or drive trucks.
The customer arranges their own truck, trailer, PODS, or container.

This distinction matters every time you speak to a customer. Never say:
- "We'll move you to your new place"
- "We'll drive your stuff"
- "Our truck"

Always say:
- "We'll load your truck"
- "We'll unload at your new address"
- "The crew"

## 2. Professional Standards

### Appearance
- Wear clean clothes on every job — no offensive graphics, torn clothing,
  or visible undergarments
- Work gloves on from the moment you start lifting
- Phone on silent unless you're expecting a call related to the job

### Punctuality
- Arrive at the agreed crew meeting point or job address **on time**
- If you're going to be late for any reason, text the owner immediately —
  never just show up late without notice
- "On time" means ready to work, not pulling into the driveway at start time

### Communication with the owner
- Text, don't call, for non-urgent updates
- Call for anything urgent: you're running late, something broke, you can't
  find the address, customer is upset
- After every job: send a quick text confirming job completion and any notes
  (damages, unusual situations, anything the owner should know)

## 3. Arrival at a Job

### First 5 minutes
1. Call or text the customer 30 minutes before arriving: "We're about
   [X] minutes away."
2. When you arrive, introduce yourself by name: "Hi, I'm [NAME] with Rest
   Assured Moving — here with the crew to help today."
3. **Do not begin unloading equipment until the customer is present or has
   explicitly told you it's OK to start.**

### Pre-job walkthrough — always, every time
Walk the entire space with the customer before touching anything:
- Confirm what moves today and what doesn't
- Ask the customer to identify any fragile or special-care items
- Note any items that need disassembly before loading
- Check stairwells, doorways, and hallways for tight spots or obstacles
- For unloading jobs: confirm which room each piece of furniture goes to
- Confirm the truck/container is accessible and in position

This walkthrough takes 5 minutes. Skipping it causes the most avoidable
problems we deal with. Never skip it.

## 4. Loading Standards

**Plan before you load:**
- Heaviest, most durable items go in first: appliances, bookcases, dressers
- Largest flat surfaces (mattresses, headboards) go against the walls
- Medium furniture fills the middle
- Boxes go on top, lightest on top
- Fragile items load last, marked clearly, protected from shifting

**Furniture wrapping — non-negotiable:**
- Every piece of furniture gets wrapped in moving blankets before it moves
- Wrap before carrying, not on the truck
- Pay extra attention to: corners, drawer fronts, mirror surfaces, legs
- Shrink wrap over blankets to hold them in place on furniture
- Nothing bare-wood against metal truck walls

**Space use:**
- Fill the truck efficiently — every cubic foot used is money saved for
  the customer
- Stack boxes tightly; loose stacks shift in transit and cause damage
- Use furniture dolly to move heavy items across floors — don't drag

**Doorways:**
- Know the width before you try to fit something through it
- Tip upright furniture when going through doorways if needed
- Never force a piece — if it's not going through, stop and reassess
- Protect doorframe edges with a moving blanket on tight carries

---

## 5. Unloading Standards

- Unload in reverse order of how things were loaded where possible
- Place every item where the customer directs — not where it's convenient
  for you
- Room-by-room placement means boxes land in the room they're labeled for
- Ask before setting anything down permanently: "Does this go here or
  somewhere else?"
- Protect floors at the destination with floor runners before bringing in
  heavy items

---

## 6. Packing Standards

**Box preparation:**
- Reinforce the bottom of every box with two strips of tape (cross pattern)
- Fill every box to the top — partial boxes collapse under weight
- Target weight: under 50 lbs; prefer under 35 lbs for a full day of carries
- Label every box clearly on the top and two sides: room + contents

**Fragile items:**
- Dishes: wrap individually in packing paper, edge-stack (never flat)
- Glasses: wrap individually, paper stuffed inside, pack upright (never inverted)
- Plates: edge-stacked, bottom and top cushioned with crumpled paper
- Mirrors and art: corner protectors, wrapped in bubble wrap, picture box,
  transported vertically
- Electronics: anti-static layer first, then bubble wrap; cables bagged
  with the device

**The shake test:**
Pick up any sealed fragile box and shake it gently. If you feel or hear
movement inside, it's not ready to load. Add more crumpled paper until
there's zero movement.

## 7. Furniture Assembly / Disassembly Standards

**Disassembly:**
- One hardware bag per component — not one bag for the whole piece
- Label each bag (masking tape + marker) before removing hardware
- Photograph complex assemblies before disassembly if helpful
- Lean disassembled panels vertically with a blanket between them —
  never flat on the floor

**Assembly:**
- Assemble in the room where the furniture will stay — not in a hallway
  to be moved later
- Hand-tighten all fasteners before fully tightening any
- Never force pieces together — if something doesn't seat easily, check
  the orientation
- Confirm everything is level and solid before moving to the next piece
- Check for leftover hardware when finished — if there's anything left,
  something may not be fully assembled

## 8. Customer Interaction

**How to be:**
- Friendly but professional — you're in someone's home
- Responsive to questions — if you don't know something, say so and get
  the owner on the phone
- Efficient without being rushed — the customer can feel the difference
  between purposeful speed and carelessness

**What to avoid:**
- Commenting on the customer's belongings (what things cost, condition, etc.)
- Using a customer's restroom without asking first
- Eating or drinking in the customer's home without an explicit offer
- Side conversations with other crew members that exclude the customer
- Being on your phone during working hours (check only on breaks)

**If a customer becomes rude or unreasonable:**
- Stay calm, stay professional
- Do not argue
- Text or call the owner and let them handle it
- You are not expected to absorb verbal abuse

---

## 9. If Something Gets Damaged

**Stop.** Before moving on to anything else:

1. Assess what happened — is the item broken, scratched, or just cosmetically marked?
2. Do not attempt to hide, minimize, or move on without addressing it
3. Inform the customer calmly and directly: "I want to let you know
   that [item] got [damaged] while we were [action]. I'm sorry about that."
4. Photograph the damage from multiple angles
5. Text the owner immediately with a brief description: "Scratched the
   leg of the oak dresser at the Salem job — customer notified, photo sent"
6. Do not make any promises about repair or compensation — that's the
   owner's decision, not yours
7. Continue the job unless the customer asks you to stop

How you handle a damage situation tells the customer more about this
company than anything else. Honesty and calm communication are the standard.

## 10. End-of-Job Protocol

**Before calling the job done:**
1. Do a complete walkthrough with the customer — every room at origin
   (nothing left behind) and destination (everything placed)
2. Ask directly: "Is there anything we missed, or anything you'd like
   us to adjust before we go?"
3. Collect any disassembled hardware and confirm it went to the right room
4. Remove all moving blankets and shrink wrap from the customer's belongings
5. Do a check of the truck or container — nothing left inside

**Payment:**
- Payment is collected at the end of the job
- You don't need to handle payment — the owner or crew lead manages this
- Never accept payment in your personal account — all payment goes through
  the business

**After leaving:**
- Text the owner: job complete, any notes (actual hours, any issues)
- Any crew equipment returns (blankets, dollies, shrink wrap) per the
  owner's instructions

---

## 11. Physical Safety

- Always lift with your legs, not your back
- Ask for help before attempting to carry something that's at the edge of
  what you can safely handle alone
- Use the dolly for anything over 60 lbs when moving across flat ground
- Use stair rollers or a team carry for heavy items on stairs
- Call out hazards: uneven pavement, wet floors, low clearances
- If you injure yourself, stop and tell the owner immediately —
  never "push through" an injury on a job

---

## 12. Equipment Responsibilities

Each crew member is responsible for:
- Handling moving blankets with care (folded, not dragged)
- Returning all equipment to the vehicle after each job
- Reporting any equipment damage or failure to the owner immediately

**Equipment check at job end:** Before leaving any job site, confirm all
blankets, dollies, shrink wrap, and tools are back in the vehicle.
Equipment left at a customer's location is expensive and embarrassing
to recover.
