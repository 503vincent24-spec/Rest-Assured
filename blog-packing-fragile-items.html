Packing Guide

How to Pack Fragile Items

By the Rest Assured Moving crew · Salem, OR

Breakage almost always comes down to one of two things: the wrong materials, or items packed loosely enough to shift in the box. Here's how our crews pack fragile items so they survive the trip.

Start with the right materials

  • Dish-pack boxes — double-walled and built specifically for plates, bowls, and glassware
  • Packing paper (not newspaper, which can transfer ink) for wrapping individual items
  • Bubble wrap for anything with a curved or delicate surface — stemware, vases, ceramics
  • Picture/mirror boxes with adjustable, telescoping sides
  • Packing tape and a tape gun, not masking tape, which doesn't hold under weight

Dishes and glassware

Wrap each plate individually in packing paper, then stack plates on their edge rather than flat — edge-stacked plates absorb shock far better than a flat stack. Wrap glasses and stemware individually, stuff the inside of each glass with crumpled paper, and place a layer of crumpled paper at the bottom and top of the box. Label the box "Fragile — Glass" on every visible side.

Mirrors, art, and framed items

Use a mirror/picture box sized to the piece, with cardboard corner protectors on all four corners. For larger or particularly valuable pieces, sandwich the item between two pieces of cardboard cut to size before boxing, and always transport mirrors and art on edge, never flat, to avoid pressure cracking.

Electronics

Original boxes are ideal if you still have them. If not, wrap screens in an anti-static layer (a clean bedsheet works in a pinch) before bubble wrap, and avoid letting packing tape touch any screen surface directly. Photograph cable setups before disconnecting anything, and pack cables in a labeled bag with the matching device.

Lamps and lampshades

Remove bulbs and pack them separately. Wrap the lamp base in bubble wrap, and pack the shade in its own box with paper stuffed loosely inside — never crushed or compressed, which permanently dents most shades.

The rule that prevents most breakage

If you can shake a packed box and hear or feel movement, it's not ready to load. Every fragile item should be packed tightly enough that nothing shifts, with cushioning material filling every gap — not just on top.
