# Social Media Campaign Strategy — Rest Assured Moving LLC

This builds on the 20 evergreen Facebook posts already delivered
(`FACEBOOK_CONTENT.txt`). Those posts are your steady-state content bank.
This document adds structure on top: a launch campaign, recurring seasonal
campaigns, a content calendar framework, platform-specific ideas (Instagram/
Reels), and local partnership/co-marketing tactics — all still bound by the
labor-only positioning rule established in the brand guide.

================================================================================
SECTION 1 — LAUNCH CAMPAIGN (First 4 Weeks on a New or Refreshed Page)
================================================================================
Goal: establish local awareness and seed early reviews/social proof.

**Week 1 — Introduce**
- Day 1: Pinned intro post (Post 1 from the evergreen bank)
- Day 3: "Meet the crew" photo/video post (even a phone-shot clip of the team
  loading a truck works better than no video at all)
- Day 5: Service spotlight — Loading & Unloading

**Week 2 — Educate**
- Day 1: Myth-busting post ("Wait, you don't drive the truck?")
- Day 3: Tip post linking to the "Preparing for Moving Day" blog post
- Day 5: Service spotlight — Furniture Assembly

**Week 3 — Prove**
- Day 1: First testimonial post (use a real one as soon as one exists;
  the placeholder testimonials on the website are illustrative only and
  should not be reused verbatim in ads/social once real reviews come in)
- Day 3: Behind-the-scenes / values post
- Day 5: Tip post linking to "How to Pack Fragile Items"

**Week 4 — Convert**
- Day 1: Direct offer — "Free quotes, book your date"
- Day 3: Service area reminder (map graphic or list of cities)
- Day 5: Closing CTA post + ask for the first round of reviews from any
  completed jobs

After week 4, fold back into the steady-state rotation described in
Section 3.

================================================================================
SECTION 2 — SEASONAL / RECURRING CAMPAIGNS
================================================================================

### Campaign A: "Summer Moving Season" (May–August)
This is peak moving season nationally and in Oregon. Increase posting
frequency to 3–4x/week and consider pairing with paid promotion (see
ADVERTISING_STRATEGY.md).
- Theme angle: "Booking fills fast in summer — lock in your crew early."
- Content ideas: countdown-style posts ("3 weeks until peak season"), crew
  capacity reminders, a UGC callout asking past customers to share photos of
  their new place.

### Campaign B: "New Year, New Place" (Late December–January)
Capitalizes on New Year's resolution energy (decluttering, fresh starts) and
January's lower moving-cost lull (good time for value-conscious movers).
- Theme angle: "Starting the year in a space that's actually put together."
- Content ideas: furniture assembly push (people buy new furniture in
  January), "declutter before you move" tips, slower-season availability
  highlight.

### Campaign C: "Back-to-School / Apartment Turnover" (August–September)
Salem and the Portland Metro area have significant student and young-renter
populations with August/September lease turnovers.
- Theme angle: "Moving into your first place? We've got the heavy lifting."
- Content ideas: dorm/apartment-specific tips (small loads, stairs, narrow
  hallways), partnership potential with local apartment complexes (see
  Section 5), student-friendly small-job framing (e.g., "no job too small —
  we'll assemble that bed frame").

### Campaign D: "Spring Refresh" (March–April)
- Theme angle: furniture assembly and home refresh projects rather than full
  moves — catches people doing spring cleaning/reorganizing.
- Content ideas: "new patio furniture? new bookshelf? we'll put it
  together," standalone-assembly push, before/after assembly photos.

SECTION 3 — STEADY-STATE CONTENT CALENDAR FRAMEWORK
================================================================================
Outside of active campaigns, rotate through these content pillars at roughly
2–3 posts/week:

| Pillar | Frequency | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Service education | 1x/week | "How we load a truck to maximize space" |
| Tips (link to blog) | 1x/week | Packing/prep/assembly tips |
| Trust & credibility | 1x/2 weeks | Pricing transparency, no-hidden-fees messaging |
| Testimonial/social proof | 1x/2 weeks | Real customer quote + photo if available |
| Local/community | 1x/2 weeks | Service area highlights, local engagement |
| Direct offer/CTA | 1x/week | Free quote push, booking reminder |

Use a simple shared spreadsheet or calendar tool (Google Sheets is fine to
start) with columns: Date, Pillar, Platform, Copy, Image/Video needed,
Status, Link.

SECTION 4 — INSTAGRAM & REELS-SPECIFIC IDEAS
================================================================================
Instagram rewards visual, short-form content more than long captions. Facebook
copy can be repurposed, but these formats perform better as their own pieces:

- **Before/after carousel**: messy truck → fully loaded, organized truck.
- **15–30 second Reel**: time-lapse or sped-up clip of a couch being wrapped
  and loaded (no need for professional video — phone footage works).
- **"Day in the life" Reel**: quick clips from one real job, captioned with
  the services performed.
- **Assembly satisfaction clip**: final screw going into a reassembled bed
  frame, crew stepping back — short, visually satisfying, easy to produce.
- **Carousel infographic**: "5 things to do before your crew arrives" reusing
  copy from the blog posts, redesigned as swipeable slides (this is a good
  use case for the Visualizer/Canva if you want help producing the actual
  slide graphics later).
- Story highlights to set up: "Services," "Reviews," "FAQ," "Service Area" —
  evergreen reference tabs on the profile.

SECTION 5 — LOCAL PARTNERSHIP & CO-MARKETING IDEAS
================================================================================
These extend reach without ad spend and reinforce the labor-only model in a
way that's mutually beneficial rather than competitive:

- **Truck rental companies (U-Haul, Penske, Budget dealers in Salem):** Ask
  about a referral arrangement — they get asked "do you know anyone who can
  help load this?" constantly, and you're the natural answer. A small stack
  of flyers or referral cards at the rental counter is a low-cost, high-
  relevance placement.
- **PODS / moving container providers:** Same logic — customers renting a
  container often want labor help and don't know where to find it locally.
- **Real estate agents:** Agents talk to people who are about to move
  constantly. A simple "preferred vendor" relationship (you refer cleaning/
  staging, they refer moving labor) can be proposed to a handful of
  Salem-area agents.
- **Apartment complexes & property managers:** Move-in/move-out season
  partnerships — offer to leave a flyer stack in the leasing office, or
  sponsor a small move-in welcome packet.
- **Storage facilities:** People moving into storage often need loading help
  on one end and may need it again later — natural overlap audience.
- **Local Nextdoor neighborhood presence:** Claim the Nextdoor Business page
  (covered further in ADVERTISING_STRATEGY.md) — word-of-mouth recommendation
  requests for "anyone know a good mover?" are common there.

================================================================================
SECTION 6 — REFERRAL PROGRAM TIE-IN
================================================================================
The referral card (`referral-card-front.svg` / `referral-card-back.svg` in
marketing-collateral) is the physical artifact for this — hand it to every
completed-job customer. Reinforce the same program digitally:
- Pin a "refer a friend, you both save $25" post monthly.
- Mention the referral program in the post-move follow-up email template
  (already in CUSTOMER_EMAIL_TEMPLATES_EXPANDED.txt) — consider adding a
  line there once the reward amount is finalized.

================================================================================
SECTION 7 — MEASUREMENT
================================================================================
Track monthly, even informally:
- Follower growth (vanity, but useful directionally)
- Engagement rate on posts (likes+comments+shares ÷ reach)
- Click-throughs to website/contact page from social (use UTM-tagged links —
  see ADVERTISING_STRATEGY.md Section on tracking)
- Quote requests that mention "saw you on Facebook/Instagram" — ask this
  directly on the quote form or verbally when booking
