# Rest Assured Moving LLC — Brand Guidelines

Moving labor company. Salem, Oregon. Loading, unloading, packing, unpacking,
furniture assembly/disassembly. **Not** a transportation, hauling, or truck
rental provider — this distinction is the single most important brand rule
and must be protected in every piece of collateral.

---

## 1. Brand Story & Positioning

People don't dread the truck rental — they dread the four hours of lifting,
wrapping, and squeezing furniture through doorways. Rest Assured Moving exists
for that part. We are the skilled hands that go with the truck, container, or
POD someone else already arranged.

**Positioning statement:** Rest Assured Moving LLC is Salem, Oregon's
moving-labor specialist — the crew that loads, unloads, packs, unpacks, and
assembles, so the move itself is one less thing to worry about.

**Brand personality:** Capable, calm, exact, warm. Think "the contractor who
shows up on time and does it right" — not "the slick national franchise."

## 2. Logo

### 2.1 Concept
**Heart Home Shield** (v2). Three ideas in one simple mark:
- **Shield:** trust, honesty, protection. *"Your belongings are safe with us."*
- **Home under a sheltering roof:** what we're really moving is someone's home.
- **Heart doorway:** care and family. *"We treat your home like family."*

No truck, van, dolly, or transportation imagery appears anywhere in the
mark. That rule was part of the original brief and still applies.

**Supporting line (cards, flyers):** *"Honest work. Treated like family."*
It's a promise about how we work. Don't call the company "family-owned"
anywhere unless that's literally true.

Earlier concepts, kept for reference: v1 shield with a checkmark doorway
(`assets/logo-archive/`), "Family Under One Roof" (people figures, which
blurred at small sizes), and "Home in Good Hands" (hands around a house).
Overview of the current mark and its versions: `assets/logo-sheet.png`.

### 2.2 Files & Variants
| File | Use |
|---|---|
| `logo-mark.svg` | Icon-only mark. Favicons, app icons, social avatars, watermarks, anywhere space is square/tight. |
| `logo-lockup.svg` | Horizontal lockup (mark + wordmark + sub-line). Website header, email signature, letterhead, invoice header. |
| `logo-mark-1color.svg` | Single-color version for embroidery (shirts, hats), vinyl decals, rubber stamps, one-ink printing. |

Both are vector (SVG) — scale losslessly from a 16px favicon to a vehicle
decal or storefront sign. Export to PNG/JPG at the specific pixel dimensions
needed per platform (see Section 7).

### 2.3 Clear Space & Minimum Size
Maintain clear space around the mark equal to the height of the shield's top
edge on all sides — don't let text or other graphics crowd it. Minimum
display size: 24px tall for the icon-only mark, 32px tall for the horizontal
lockup, below which the checkmark detail starts to lose legibility.

### 2.4 Don'ts
- Don't recolor the shield or house outside the approved palette.
- Don't add a truck, van, or wheels to the mark anywhere, in any context.
- Don't stretch or skew the mark — scale proportionally only.
- Don't place the navy mark on a dark background without swapping to the
  light/white version (a reversed variant should be produced before any
  dark-background print use — see Section 8 limitations).

## 3. Color Palette

| Swatch | Name | Hex | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| 🟦 | Navy 900 | `#0F2547` | Primary brand color. Headlines, shield fill (dark), footer backgrounds. |
| 🟦 | Navy 800 | `#16335E` | Secondary navy, gradients, hover states. |
| 🟩 | Emerald 700 | `#11533D` | Deep accent, hover state for emerald elements. |
| 🟩 | Emerald 600 | `#1F7A5C` | Primary accent. CTAs, checkmarks, links, highlights. |
| 🟩 | Emerald 500 | `#2E9874` | Lighter accent for dark backgrounds (text/icons on navy). |
| ⬜ | Gray 100 | `#EFF2F0` | Light surface backgrounds, alternating sections. |
| ⬜ | Gray 200 | `#E2E6E3` | Borders, dividers, dotted rules. |
| ⬜ | Paper | `#FAFAF8` | Page/background base — warm off-white, not stark white. |
| ⬛ | Ink | `#222B36` | Primary body text. |

**Usage ratio guideline:** roughly 60% paper/gray neutrals, 30% navy, 10%
emerald used as an accent/action color — not as a background flood. Emerald
signals "go" (CTAs, checkmarks, confirmations); navy signals authority and
trust; gray keeps everything calm and uncluttered.

**Accessibility:** Navy 900 on Paper and White on Navy 900 both exceed
WCAG AA contrast for body text. Emerald 600 on White passes AA for large
text/UI elements; avoid small emerald text on white for body copy — use it
for headings, icons, or buttons with white text instead.

## 4. Typography

| Role | Typeface | Fallback stack | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Display / Headlines | Georgia (or Lora if available) | `Georgia, 'Iowan Old Style', 'Times New Roman', serif` | H1–H4, the wordmark, pull quotes. Conveys the "established, trustworthy" feel. |
| Body | System UI sans | `-apple-system, BlinkMacSystemFont, 'Segoe UI', Helvetica, Arial, sans-serif` | Paragraphs, navigation, buttons, forms. |
| Utility / Labels | Monospace | `'Courier New', ui-monospace, monospace` | Eyebrows, manifest/checklist labels, stamps, fine print — the "ledger" texture that ties to the checkmark motif. |

**Why a serif display face for a labor/trades business?** It's a deliberate
contrast play — most moving-labor competitors lean on bold sans-serif and
truck-yellow. A confident serif borrowed from the insurance/legal world
reinforces "rest assured" as a literal promise, not just a name.

**Hierarchy rule of thumb:** Serif for anything that should feel like a
statement or a promise (headlines, the brand name). Sans for anything that
should feel efficient and easy to scan (body copy, lists, forms). Monospace
for anything that should feel like a record or a receipt (labels, stamps,
manifest items).

## 5. Voice & Messaging

**Tone:** Plain-spoken, specific, and warm — never salesy, never jokey at the
customer's expense (moving is stressful for them).

**Always:**
- Say "crew" or "movers," never "drivers."
- Attribute the truck/trailer/POD/container to the customer ("your truck,"
  "the container you've booked") — never imply Rest Assured Moving owns or
  operates a vehicle.
- Lead with clarity about scope before selling on benefit ("We load, unload,
  pack, unpack, and assemble — you handle the truck").
- Use concrete numbers over vague claims ("crew of 2–4," "flat hourly rate")
  rather than "affordable" or "fast."

**Avoid:**
- "We'll get you there," "door to door," "delivery," "transport," "haul,"
  "shipping," or any phrase implying movement between locations.
- Generic moving-industry clichés ("stress-free move," "moving made easy")
  used without backing them up with a specific, concrete detail.

### Slogans / Taglines
Primary: **"Rest assured, it's handled."**

Supporting / contextual alternates:
- "The crew for the heavy lifting."
- "You bring the truck. We bring the muscle."
- "Loaded right. Unloaded right. Put back together right."
- "Moving labor, done like it matters."

## 6. Signature Visual Element: The Move Manifest

A recurring "checklist/ledger card" component — dashed rule lines, a
checkbox-style checkmark bullet, monospace labels like job numbers and
statuses — appears across the website, social templates, and print
collateral. It visually echoes the logo's checkmark and gives the brand a
consistent, ownable motif beyond just "navy and green," distinguishing it
from generic moving-company visuals (boxes, trucks, tape).

Use it for: service checklists, testimonial framing, "what's included" lists,
quote summaries, and social post templates.

## 8. Known Limitations / Next Steps

- **Raster exports now exist** for the listings: `assets/social/*.png` and
  `assets/og-image.png`, rendered from `tools/social-images.html`. The notes
  below still apply to any *other* PNG you need (business cards, decals).
  Previously: All
  logo and business card files are delivered as SVG. SVG is the better
  *source* format regardless, but you'll want PNG exports for platforms that
  don't accept SVG uploads (Facebook profile photo, Google Business Profile
  logo, some print portals). Any of the following will convert SVG → PNG in
  under a minute: dropping the file into Figma/Illustrator and exporting,
  using a free tool like cloudconvert.com or svgtopng.com, or opening the SVG
  in a browser and using a screenshot/export extension.
- **A reversed (white/light) version of the icon-only mark** for dark
  backgrounds hasn't been produced yet — flag this if you need the mark on
  dark merchandise, apparel, or signage beyond what's already covered (the
  business card back already includes a watermark treatment as one example).
- **Vehicle/equipment decals or apparel** aren't covered in this guide yet —
  let me know if you want a one-color (single-emerald or single-navy) version
  of the mark prepped for vinyl cutting or embroidery digitizing.
