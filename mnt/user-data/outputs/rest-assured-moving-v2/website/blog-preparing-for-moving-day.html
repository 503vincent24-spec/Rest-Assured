Moving Day Prep

10 Tips for Preparing for Moving Day

By the Rest Assured Moving crew — Salem, Oregon

Most moving-day stress doesn't come from the actual move — it comes from everything that wasn't decided, packed, or arranged in the days leading up to it. These ten steps, worked through in order starting about two weeks out, are what separate a smooth moving day from a chaotic one.

1. Lock in your truck or container at least two weeks out

Rental trucks, PODS, and moving containers book up fast — especially around the 1st and last weekend of each month, which are the most popular move dates. Reserve yours as early as possible. When choosing a size, err toward one tier larger than you think you need: running out of space mid-load is far more expensive than an extra few feet of unused truck.

2. Book your moving labor crew at the same time

A common mistake is booking the truck first, then scrambling for loading help. The popular dates your moving crew wants are the same dates everyone else wants. If you're using a moving labor company like Rest Assured Moving, request a quote and lock in your crew as soon as your truck date is confirmed — not after.

3. Do a serious "keep, donate, discard" pass three weeks out

Every item you move costs time and space in the truck. Before you pack a single box, walk every room and make three stacks honestly. Items you haven't used in a year, furniture that won't fit your new space, duplicates — these are all candidates to donate, sell, or discard. It's far easier to do this before boxes are sealed and labeled than after they're already on the truck.

4. Collect boxes early; buy specialty boxes once

Liquor stores, bookstores, and grocery stores often have sturdy free boxes — call ahead, as they tend to break them down quickly. Save your budget for boxes that are worth buying: dish-pack boxes (double-walled, designed for plates and bowls), wardrobe boxes with hanging bars, and adjustable picture/mirror boxes. These specialty sizes prevent damage that free boxes cannot.

5. Pack a first-night bag and keep it with you

Every moving day ends with you in a new place surrounded by unpacked boxes, exhausted. The items you'll need immediately — phone charger, medications, toiletries, a change of clothes, a towel, your coffee maker, and important documents — should travel with you, not in the truck. Pack them in a clearly labeled bag or box that stays in your car, not on the truck. This single step saves more post-move frustration than almost anything else on this list.

6. Label boxes by room and contents — not just category

"Kitchen — pots and pans, cabinet above the stove" is a useful label. "Misc kitchen" is not. Labels that tell your unloading crew both the room and the general contents mean boxes land in the right place immediately, which cuts unpacking time significantly. Write on at least two sides and the top of every box so it's readable in a stack.

7. Disassemble large furniture the evening before

Bed frames, large bookshelves, and sectionals almost always need to come apart to fit safely through doorways and into a truck. Doing this the night before, with hardware bagged and labeled per piece, saves significant time on moving morning. If you'd rather not handle it yourself, furniture disassembly is a service your moving labor crew can add to the job — just let them know in advance so they arrive with the right tools.

8. Measure doorways at both addresses

A couch that fits through your current front door won't necessarily fit through your new one. Large furniture pieces — sectionals, king bed frames, oversized dressers — should be measured before moving day, and the doorways, stairwells, and hallways at both the origin and destination should be measured as well. A five-minute measuring pass now prevents a costly standoff in a stairwell on moving day.

9. Clear a path before your crew arrives

The single biggest factor in how fast a loading job goes — after crew size — is how clear the path is from your belongings to the truck. Before your moving labor crew arrives, move rugs out of hallways, relocate plants and coatstands, prop doors open, and reserve a parking space for the truck as close to your entrance as possible. This preparation directly reduces the billable hours on your job.

10. Confirm everything the day before

Call or confirm via email/text: your truck rental pickup time, your moving labor crew's arrival window, and your elevator reservation if you're in a building that requires one. Moving day has enough variables — the truck being at a different pickup location, the crew arriving at a different door, or an elevator that's booked by another resident at the time you need it are all avoidable surprises with a five-minute round of confirmation calls.
